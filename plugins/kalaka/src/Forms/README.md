# Forms

Forms, the registration slot, field types, field definitions, values and conditions, prefill and form links.

A form is a `kalaka_form` post (registered in `Module.php` with its settings as post meta: `kalaka_event_id`, `kalaka_placement` and `kalaka_required_role`; the password is the post password). Its content is the definition, block markup built from the field blocks. Forms are built in the block editor, by full access only: the post type has `show_ui` and `show_in_rest` but no menu item, and `FormRestController` refuses every REST read without `kalaka_full_access`; the settings are in REST for the same users. A form's version is the ID of its latest revision that is not an autosave; WordPress saves none on insert, so `FormPosts::ensureRevision()` saves the first one, and revisions of forms are never pruned.

On every save (`wp_insert_post_data`), `FieldKeys` sets each field's missing key from its title and each choice option's missing value from its label, with `KeyDerivation`; a derived key avoids every key the form's event ever had (`kalaka_participant_fields`), and a block that arrives without a key first takes back the key of the stored field of its type and title that no incoming block holds (the editor does not re-read the keys the server set). `FormEvent` tells a save which event the form has: the one the REST request or `meta_input` sets, else the stored one; once a form has fields its event is fixed, and the meta filters refuse a change on any path. Then `DefinitionCheck` refuses a change of the event of a form with fields (`event_fixed`) and runs `ParticipantField\SharedKeyCheck` on every save but an autosave (a key twice in the form, `duplicate_key`; a shared key of another type, `type_clash`; a `system` field's key, `system_field`; an `extension` field's key unless the extension declared it form-writable, `extension_field`), and the live form's definition check on a form saved as published or scheduled (and again on `publish_future_post`, before WordPress publishes a scheduled form: a broken one goes back to draft and its author gets the notice): the REST save is refused with a 400 (`rest_pre_insert_kalaka_form`), and any other save path (code, WP-CLI, a revision restore) is vetoed gracefully in the filter (a published form keeps its stored content, any other form stays unpublished) with an admin notice for the saving user; nothing throws out of a core filter. `BrokenForms` keeps the list of published forms whose definition is broken (saved before the check, or written around it) in a non-autoloaded option, recomputed when a form changes, and `BrokenFormsWarning` shows it as a site warning; such a form stays closed.

`BlockGate` (on `allowed_block_types_all`) gates the blocks by post type: a `kalaka_form` editor gets an allow-list (the field types' blocks, `FORM_ONLY_BLOCKS`, `CONDITION_BLOCKS` and `FORM_CORE_BLOCKS`), and any other editor gets the gated blocks (the field types' blocks and `FORM_ONLY_BLOCKS`) only when a `BlockHost` names them: an incoming list is narrowed, an allow-all `true` stays `true` and the editor script unregisters the gated blocks on the client (`Blocks/form/hidden.js`, from the names `BlockGate::enqueueHiding()` prints before it on `enqueue_block_editor_assets` as `window.kalakaHiddenBlocks`). The condition blocks are not gated: every editor offers them. There is no `ancestor`.

Once the post and its settings are stored (`wp_after_insert_post`, which a trash and a restore fire too), or once a form is deleted for good (`before_delete_post` keeps its event, `deleted_post` syncs), `ParticipantFieldSync` reads the fields of every form of the event that is not trashed (`ParticipantField\FieldBlocks`) and declares them through `ParticipantFields\Api\ParticipantFields::declare()` (the use case `DeclareParticipantFields` of the `ParticipantFields` module), as the saving user: it declares new keys (the field's title and option labels are the ones it is first declared with, and stay the field's own), unions choice options by value and retires the fields and options no form holds any more. A form field that clashes with the event's field (only a form written around the save check) is left out and logged, and the rest is declared; `Forms::definition()` refuses the current version of such a form (`type_clash`, `system_field`, `extension_field`), so a published one is closed and listed by `BrokenForms`. Fields are listed in the order they were declared (`position`). `Forms::participantFields()` is `ParticipantFields::of()` with, per field, the forms of the event that hold it now (`ParticipantField::withForms()`). The table, its records and the two use cases belong to the `ParticipantFields` module; `ParticipantField/` here holds `FieldBlocks` and `SharedKeyCheck`, which read the event's fields through `ParticipantFields::of()`, retired ones included. `ParticipantFields::rename()` (`RenameParticipantField`) is the only change of a field's title or option labels after they are declared, for a `form` or `staff` field; `ParticipantFieldsRoute` serves it as `PATCH kalaka-admin/v1/events/{id}/participant-fields/{key}` with `{title?, options?}`, for the form editor's "update the field's text too?" and the field list.

At the root: `FormPosts` (reads the posts, their revisions and their settings), `DefinitionParser` (reads the field blocks, in document order, out of any core blocks and condition branches around them, numbers the chains and collects the triggers), `SlotRules` (the registration slot's markings, none of them inside a condition branch), `FormWalk` with `FormFacts` and `FormWalkResult` (the one walk of a form over answers: what shows, which branch of each chain holds, which options are offered, the valid answers and the errors), `AnswerValidator` (the server's check of answers: the walk, then any error refuses them), `DefinitionCheck` (the save check), `FormDeletion` (a form with submissions is never deleted permanently), `BrokenForms` and `BrokenFormsWarning`, `BlockGate`, `KeyDerivation` and `FieldKeys` (keys and option values), `FormRestController`, `FieldMarkup` (the field blocks' shared markup) and `ThemeAdapters` (picks the theme adapter once per request).

`Api/` holds `Forms` (the facade: `form()`, `definition()`, `latestDefinition()`, `blocks()`, `refusals()`, `validate()`, `availability()`, `participantFields()`), `Form`, `FormPlacement`, `FormDefinition`, `FormField`, `FormBranch`, `FormChain`, `FormAvailability` (what a form shows for its answers, the front end's read model), `AccessRefusal`, `AfterSubmit` (the after-submit section's block and its value keys), `PageDefinition` (a page of a multi-page form), `FormTerminal` (a submit while a terminal page shows), `FormOutdated` (a page built from a version that is no longer the latest), `FormRefused` (a form that cannot be used now, with the refusal codes), `FormCaching` (a page that places a form is never cached), `ValidatedAnswers`, `InvalidAnswers`, the availability base (`Availability`, `Conditional` and the fact source `Facts`), the event keys (`EventValues`), the value API (`Values`, the facade with `render()`, `Value`, `ValueType` and `ValueContext`) and the field-type point: `FieldType`, `AbstractFieldType`, `FieldTypeInput`, `FieldAnswer` and the optional `MoneyRelevantFieldType`. Field types are contributed with `Contributions::fieldType()`. The theme-adapter point: `ThemeAdapter`, `ThemeAdapterInput`, `ThemeAdaptation`, `ElementRole` and `FieldWrapper`, contributed with `Contributions::themeAdapter()`. `BlockHost`, a declaration contributed with `Contributions::blockHost()`.

`FieldType/` holds the seven core types: `NameFieldType`, `EmailFieldType`, `PhoneFieldType`, `DateFieldType`, `TextFieldType`, `ChoiceFieldType` and `ConsentFieldType`. `Blocks/<type>-field/` holds each type's `block.json` (title, the `kalaka` category, no `ancestor`), `render.php` (through `FieldMarkup`) and `edit.js`. Every block is registered from `build/` (`npm run build` builds and copies the block files there), by `Module::register()`. The editor is one script, `Blocks/form/index.js` (handle `kalaka-form-editor-script`, which every Kaláka block's `block.json` names): it registers each block's `edit.js`, keeps field keys and option values stable (`Blocks/form/keys.js`), reads the field-type blocks, the condition keys and a date reference's bounds from the server (`Blocks/form/server.js`, printed by `Module::editorScriptData()`), and imports the structural CSS (`Blocks/form/style.css`, the form block's `style`).

`Condition/` holds the stored condition format: `ConditionParser` reads it into a `Condition` (made of `Comparison`s, `DateReference`s, `Operator`s and `MatchMode`s) for the keys a `ConditionScope` allows (`FormScope` in a form, `ContextScope` over a fact source anywhere else, `DeclaredScope` over declared keys when a condition is saved), `ConditionEvaluator` decides whether it holds (pure; the one evaluator), `Chains` finds the `kalaka/if`, `kalaka/else-if` and `kalaka/else` chains among sibling blocks, and `ChainMarks` numbers them once on the parsed blocks (chain number and branch index as block attributes), for the definition and the rendered page alike.

`Value/` holds the renderer behind `Values::render()` (a mail body; the first failure stops it) and `Values::renderPage()` (an ordinary page, whose context is the visitor's active registration and its event; a failing chain or value renders nothing and is logged): `ChainPass` keeps the held branch of every chain, `BindingSource` is the Block Bindings source `kalaka/value`, `InlineValues` fills `<span data-kalaka-value="key">` with the HTML API, and `RenderStack` holds the context of the render in progress. `Blocks/after-submit/` holds the after-submit section, which renders nothing where it sits. `Blocks/form-page/` holds the page block, rendered by `PageMarkup` (a hidden wrapper with its number, a terminal mark and its title) in a form page and nothing elsewhere. Both have an `edit.js` that the form editor registers. `Blocks/if/`, `Blocks/else-if/` and `Blocks/else/` hold the three branch blocks; through `BranchMarkup` they render their inner blocks, with no wrapper, only for the held branch inside those calls; in a form page's marked blocks (`Forms::blocks()`) every branch of a chain that reads only the form's own answers, each in a hidden wrapper with `data-kalaka-chain` and `data-kalaka-branch`, and of any other chain only its held branch (`FormDefinition::withheldChains()`, `ChainMarks::prune()`); nothing anywhere else. Nothing reads the active registration yet, so no page calls `renderPage()` with a context: the slice that adds `ActiveRegistration::current()` wires it.

`Api\Forms` reads WordPress posts, so it runs before or after a use case's transaction, never inside it.

The form page: `Blocks/form/` is the embed block `kalaka/form` (`block.json`, `render.php` with its texts, the script module `view.js` with `answers.js`, the reader of a field's controls, and `view.css`, the loading state, the error colour of field messages and the required asterisk, and a rule that keeps a hidden Vissza, Tovább or Küldés button hidden where a theme gives buttons a `display` of their own (Divi's `.et_pb_button`), registered from `src/` as the handle `kalaka-form-view`). The shortcode `[kalaka_form id="…"]` (`Module::shortcode()`) renders the same block; both call `Api\FormCaching::preventCaching()`. `FormPage` decides whether the form shows (the load state first, then the form, its event, `Forms::refusals()`, the definition) and renders the marked blocks of `Forms::blocks()` for no answers from `Forms::availability()` (S76), inside one `Boundary::render()`: the branch of each chain that shows is unhidden, the controls of a field that does not show are disabled, a choice field renders only its offered options, the event values (`Api/EventValues`, the one place that declares the event keys) fill the bindings and inline values, and a field block of an extension without the error element (`FieldMarkup::errorElement()`) gets one. The form carries the theme adapter's form classes and error colour (`--kalaka-error-color`) and its buttons the adapter's submit-button classes. The script re-fetches through the re-fetch route and sends through the submit route of `Rest\FormRoutes` (see the entry points). `FormPageView` is the page's result: one refusal or the content and the form element's context (the URLs, the version, the failure text, `nonce`, the triggers and the pages). `nonce` is `wp_create_nonce('wp_rest')` for a logged-in person and empty for a guest; the script sends it as `X-WP-Nonce` on the re-fetch and the submit, so the routes see the account. The script (`view.js`) holds no condition: on a change of a trigger it re-fetches and applies the answer (the chains' branches, the fields' disabled controls, the re-rendered choice fields with the remembered ticks, the pages that show); on a multi-page form it shows the current page, checks it with a re-fetch that sends `page` on Tovább, moves back on Vissza and on the browser's Back button (`history.pushState`, scoped to the form), and after a refused submit goes to the earliest page with an error or to the terminal page that shows. While a re-fetch, a page check or a send runs the form is `aria-busy` (dimmed) and its buttons `aria-disabled`, and after 2 seconds the spinner shows; after a failed re-fetch the form is out of date, and Tovább or Küldés re-runs the re-fetch. Only Kaláka's own errors (a `kalaka_` code or messages of fields) show their message; a field's message goes into its error element, which describes its controls (`aria-describedby`, `aria-invalid`), and the focus moves to the first field with a message, the message area, the page's title or the after-submit content. `DateText` makes the text of a day (`2026. november 30.`). `FieldBlockCheck` closes the site, through `LoadState::close()`, when a published form holds a `kalaka/` block that is not registered. `Cli/FormSeeder` seeds the sample form and the page that embeds it.

Entry points: `init` (`Module::register()`), `wp_loaded` (`FieldBlockCheck::run()`), `ParticipantFieldsRoute`, `GET kalaka-admin/v1/events/{id}/participant-fields` for the form editor (full access only, `include_retired` to add the retired fields), the block `kalaka/form`, `wp kalaka seed` (`Cli/FormSeeder`); the form page's routes in `Rest\FormRoutes`, registered on `rest_api_init` through `Boundary::route()`. `POST kalaka-portal/v1/forms/{id}/availability` `{version, answers, page?}` answers `{availability: FormAvailability::toArray(), parts: {key: html}}`, where `parts` are the choice fields with option conditions re-rendered for the answers (`ChoiceParts`); it applies the form's refusals (`FormRefused`) and reads only. `POST kalaka-portal/v1/forms/{id}/submit` `{version, answers, token}` runs `SubmitGuard` (a no-op until spam protection, S14) and `RegistrationSubmit::submit()` as the logged-in user (`AccountActor`, with their roles) or a guest, and answers `SubmitResult::toArray()`. `FormRoutes::noStore()` on `rest_request_after_callbacks` sets `Cache-Control: no-store` on every answer of both.
